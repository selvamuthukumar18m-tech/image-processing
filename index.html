Classical Sliding-Window Approach

1. Template Matching

Mathematical sliding window formulation, correlation metrics, interactive canvas visualizer, and transformation failure modes.

Mathematical Formulations

Template matching moves a template patch $T$ of dimensions $w \times h$ across an input image $I$ of size $W \times H$. At every sub-window $(x,y)$, a similarity metric $R(x,y)$ is evaluated.

1. Squared Difference (TM_SQDIFF_NORMED)
$$R_{SSD}(x,y) = \frac{\sum_{x',y'} (T(x',y') - I(x+x', y+y'))^2}{\sqrt{\sum (T)^2 \cdot \sum (I)^2}}$$

Optimal match occurs at local minimum ($\text{Score} \to 0$).

2. Normalized Cross-Correlation (TM_CCORR_NORMED)
$$R_{CCORR}(x,y) = \frac{\sum_{x',y'} T(x',y') \cdot I(x+x', y+y')}{\sqrt{\sum (T)^2 \cdot \sum (I)^2}}$$

Optimal match occurs at local maximum ($\text{Score} \to 1$).

3. Correlation Coefficient (TM_CCOEFF_NORMED)
$$R_{CCOEFF}(x,y) = \frac{\sum (T' \cdot I')}{\sqrt{\sum (T')^2 \cdot \sum (I')^2}}$$

Subtracts local mean $T'(x',y') = T(x',y') - \bar{T}$, providing invariance to global uniform brightness shifts.

Critical Limitations in Video Analytics

While mathematically straightforward, template matching fails in real-world intelligent video surveillance due to severe lack of invariance:

  • Scale Invariance: Even a 10% change in subject distance from camera destroys pixel correlation; requires expensive multi-resolution pyramids.
  • In-Plane & Out-of-Plane Rotation: Cannot accommodate face pose changes, yaw, pitch, or head tilts without checking every rotation angle individually.
  • Deformation & Occlusion: Facial expressions (smiling, blinking) alter local topology, resulting in false negatives.
  • Computational Complexity: Naive time complexity is $O((W-w)(H-h) \cdot wh)$, making high-definition video scanning intractable without FFT acceleration.

2. Viola-Jones Algorithm

Haar-like features, $O(1)$ Integral Image rectangle sums, AdaBoost feature boosting, and the Attentional Cascade.

1

Haar-like Features

Simple rectangular difference operators: Edge, Line, and Diagonal features reflecting facial anatomy (e.g. eyes darker than cheeks).

2

Integral Image

Intermediate image representation computing the sum of arbitrary sub-rectangles in constant $O(1)$ time with only 4 array lookups.

3

AdaBoost Boosting

Selects a critical subset of ~6,000 discriminative features from an exhaustive pool of >160,000 features while constructing weak classifiers.

4

Attentional Cascade

Funnel architecture that rapidly rejects >95% of non-face background patches in early stages using simple classifiers, reserving complex models for candidates.

3. DeepFace

Closing the gap to human performance (97.35% on LFW) via 3D Face Alignment & Locally Connected Layers.

The Core Architectural Insight

Standard Convolutional Neural Networks assume translational invariance (parameter sharing across the spatial grid). In general object detection (e.g. ImageNet), a dog can appear anywhere in an image.

Why Locally Connected Layers?

Once a face is rigidly aligned in 3D, eyes are always at specific spatial coordinates, and the mouth is always at the bottom.

Sharing weights forces a convolution filter to simultaneously model eyes and mouths. DeepFace solves this using Locally Connected Layers (layers L4, L5, L6): each spatial location has its own independent filter weights without spatial weight sharing!

Trained on the Social Face Classification (SFC) dataset: 4.4 million labeled faces belonging to 4,030 identities. Verification uses the 4,096-D representation at layer F7.

4. FaceNet

Direct 128-D Euclidean space embedding, Triplet Loss optimization, and online semi-hard negative mining.

The Triplet Loss Formulation

Unlike DeepFace which trained on intermediate classification and extracted bottleneck activations, FaceNet directly optimizes an embedding function $f(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$ such that squared Euclidean distance corresponds to facial similarity:

Triplet Loss Objective
$$\mathcal{L} = \sum_{i}^{N} \max\left(0, \|f(x_i^a) - f(x_i^p)\|_2^2 - \|f(x_i^a) - f(x_i^n)\|_2^2 + \alpha\right)$$

Where $x_i^a$ is an Anchor image, $x_i^p$ is a Positive (same person), $x_i^n$ is a Negative (different person), and $\alpha$ is the margin constraint.

L2 Normalization Constraint
$$\|f(x)\|_2 = 1 \implies f(x) \text{ lies on unit hypersphere } \mathbb{S}^{127}$$

The Three Triplet Regimes & Mining

Selecting triplets at random produces vanishing gradients. FaceNet categorizes triplets into three mathematical categories:

1. Easy Triplets:
$\|x_a - x_n\|^2 > \|x_a - x_p\|^2 + \alpha$

Loss is exactly 0. Gives zero gradient; useless for backprop training.

2. Semi-Hard Triplets (Optimal Target):
$\|x_a - x_p\|^2 < \|x_a - x_n\|^2 < \|x_a - x_p\|^2 + \alpha$

Negative is further than positive, but still inside margin $\alpha$. Produces stable, non-collapsing gradient descent!

3. Hard Triplets:
$\|x_a - x_n\|^2 < \|x_a - x_p\|^2$

Negative is closer than positive. Risky early on: can cause bad local minima or collapsed embeddings.
